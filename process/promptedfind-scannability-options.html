Prompted Find — making the page scannable

The problem, in your words: the content is too much for a recruiter to scan and get the idea. The video and stepper carry the visual story; the six prose sections fight it — ~600 words, all in the same gray paragraph voice, and three sections repeat the same “pattern beyond video” point. Below: the prioritized findings, then three treatments rendered in the page’s real type and colors so you can judge them in context. The options are not exclusive — A and B combine naturally.

P1 Structural — changes what a scanner actually reads
  • 1
    There is no at-a-glance layer. After the hero, every fact on the page lives inside a full paragraph. A recruiter’s first pass is 30–60 seconds; right now that pass yields the title, the video, and gray text blocks.
  • 2
    The same meta-point is made three times. “The medium changes; the interaction stays the same” appears in The Method, again in the “why this pattern matters” note, and again as One Pattern Three Surfaces. Keep the strip (it’s visual), fold the other two into it. Saves ~120 words.
  • 3
    “What I’d Change” repeats “What’s real, what’s simulated.” The real-embeddings point is made in both. Keep it in the honesty note, cut the third reflection bullet. (Already on the punch list as P1.)
P2 Copy density — cut ~40% without losing the story
  • 4
    Paragraphs run 3–4 sentences with double line breaks. Nearly every section reduces to one ink-colored claim plus one gray supporting line. The Gap’s pullquote and its body text also say the same thing twice — keep whichever reads better, not both.
  • 5
    The Method opens the body with the most abstract text on the page. A scanner meets “Semantic Decomposition: break long-form content into labeled, navigable semantic units” before they’ve seen the concrete thing. Either compress it to one line, or let the concrete Gap section come first and name the method after.
P3 Craft details — quick wins, do alongside whichever option
  • 6
    Stepper auto-advances every 3.5s with no pause on hover. It’s the one element a reader studies closely, and it moves while they read. Pause while hovered or focused; resume on leave.
  • 7
    Type floor: pipeline labels are 10px, stepper tab numbers 11px — below the 11–12px floor the rest of the page respects.
  • 8
    The meta-row link hover isn’t gated behind (hover:hover) like every other hover on the page — taps on phones trigger a stuck hover state.
A Facts bar — give the scanner a landing strip Recommended, combined with B

One four-cell strip under the hero descriptor answers the recruiter’s first four questions — what is it, what did you do, what’s real, where’s the pattern going — before any paragraph. Everything below it becomes optional depth instead of required reading.

+ Biggest scan payoff for the smallest change; nothing else has to move.  − Adds one more boxed element to a page that already has four.

B Bold lead — every section scannable by its first line Recommended, combined with A

Each section opens with one ink-colored claim set slightly larger; the gray support text shrinks to one or two sentences. A scanner who reads only the bold lines gets the whole story; a reader who wants depth still has it. This is where the ~40% copy cut lands.

+ Fixes density everywhere at once, no new components, keeps the current structure.  − Requires a real copy pass — it’s editing work, not layout work.

The Gap

Video is easy to watch and hard to search: a document has headings, a video only has time.

Prompted Find makes the timeline navigable by meaning. Ask what you want to find; the answer is a timestamp, not a transcript.

The Method

Semantic Decomposition: split anything long into labeled units, so a question can return a location.

Prompted Find is the method applied to video. The same move works on prose, podcasts, and research trails.

C Visual-first — prose becomes captions beside the artifacts Bigger swing

Lean fully into “the video and stepper carry the story”: text moves next to the visuals as side captions, and the page becomes a sequence of artifacts — video, stepper, pipeline, surfaces strip — each with a short caption column. Almost nothing left to “read,” everything to look at.

+ Most distinctive; strongest match to how a design-eng audience actually browses.  − Real layout surgery: side captions need a second column that collapses on mobile, and section order has to be rebuilt. Riskier for a page that’s already live and working.
