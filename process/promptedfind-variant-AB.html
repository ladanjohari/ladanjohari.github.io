The Gap

Video is easy to watch and hard to search. A document has headings; a video only has time.

Prompted Find makes the timeline navigable by meaning. Ask what you want to find, and the answer is a timestamp you can trust, not a transcript or a summary.

The Method

Semantic Decomposition: split anything long into labeled units, so a question can return a location.

Prompted Find is the method applied to video. The same move works on any surface with length but no structure: prose, podcasts, research trails.

Decisions

Three choices define what kind of interface this is.

01

The answer is a timestamp, not text

“Search as navigation.”

The query moves you inside the video. The interface doesn't summarize or transcribe. It relocates you to the moment where the answer lives.

02

Segments, not a search results list

“The timeline is the interface.”

The timeline is divided into labeled semantic segments. Relevant ones highlight. You see the shape of the answer before you click it.

03

The query stays visible while you watch

“Context persists.”

The question panel stays open alongside the video, so follow-ups never cost you your place.

The Interaction

Three steps, and none of them take you out of the video.

How It Works

A 74-minute NASA talk, nine labeled segments, four stages between the question and the moment.

Ask
A natural-language question. “How do they slow down?”
Expand
Concept aliases broaden the query. “EDL” also means entry, descent, landing.
Score
Every segment’s label and summary is scored against the expanded terms.
Locate
Matching ranges highlight on the timeline. Clicking one seeks the video.

What’s real, what’s simulated. The interface, timeline, and navigation are working code. The understanding layer is concept aliasing and keyword scoring, not a model. A production version would swap in semantic embeddings; the interaction wouldn’t change.

What I'd Change

  • Confidence indicators. Not all matches are equally strong. The timeline should visually encode match confidence so the user can prioritize where to jump.
  • Multi-video support. The real value is cross-video search: “find the part about heat shields” across an entire lecture series.
