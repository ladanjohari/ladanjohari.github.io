Prompted Find

Ask a question about a video. Land on the moment that answers it.

2025 / Coded prototype · HTML and JS / GitHub →

Working prototype, running on a 74 minute NASA talk.

Why it matters

A document has headings, paragraphs, a table of contents. Video only has time.

So when the thing you need is somewhere inside 74 minutes, your only tool is the scrubber. You drag, you guess, you overshoot, you drag back. Prompted Find replaces that with a question.

It’s for anyone who has to pull information out of recorded talks: students, researchers, and anyone catching up on a meeting they missed.

The idea

Semantic decomposition. Break long content into labeled units of meaning, so a question can return a location instead of a summary.

A transcript gives you every word and no shape. A summary gives you shape and takes away the source. Labeled segments give you both, and they give you somewhere to point.

How it works

Where else it works

Anything long with no structure: podcasts, recorded meetings, interviews, legal proceedings. The medium changes. The interaction stays the same.

What’s next

  • Confidence on the timeline. Not every match is equally strong, and the timeline should show which are worth your time.
  • Across a whole series. Ask once, search every video in a lecture series.
  • Real embeddings. The interface is finished code. The understanding layer is simulated and drops out for real embeddings without changing a single interaction.
