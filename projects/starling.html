Starling

Too many tabs? One press, and your browser tidies its own tabs into groups.

2026 / Coded prototype · Swift and WebKit / Watch the demo →
Try it yourself.

Why it matters

Too many tabs is so common that it has its own name, tab hoarding, and its own section on Wikipedia.

Finding one tab means reading tiny titles one by one, and sorting them by hand takes longer than it is worth. So nobody does it.

Design decisions

Today: 20 tabs in a row. Every title is gone.

Organize sits under the tabs, with its name on it.

The toolbar is for the page you are looking at: back, reload, the address. Organize acts on the list of tabs, so it sits right under that list. It carries its name because an icon alone does not say what it does.

Organize

Groups you name stay yours.

When you organize again, Starling re-sorts only the groups it made. A group you named stays exactly as you left it, with the same tabs inside.

How it works

Organize does the sorting you would do by hand.

By hand
Read every title, decide the groups, drag each tab, type each name.
With Starling
One press. About a second.

Why two steps and not one: in a test with 43 tabs, asking a single model to do the whole job took 8 to 19 seconds, and the groups were no better. Grouping first and naming second takes about a second.

More applications

The same button could work on anything that piles up and never gets sorted: downloads, screenshots, saved links.
