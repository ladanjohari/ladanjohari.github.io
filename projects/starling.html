Too many tabs? One press, and your browser tidies its own tabs into groups.

Try it. Shift-click Organize to say how.

Working prototype, running on 16 real tabs. Click a step.

Why it matters

Every browser lets tabs pile up. None of them puts them away for you.

After a day of research there are forty tabs open, and finding one means reading tiny titles one by one. Sorting them by hand takes longer than the research did, so nobody does it.

It’s for people who think in the browser: designers collecting references, developers reading documentation, anyone planning a trip.

The idea

Sort by meaning, name in words. A small model reads each page and places it by what it is about, so similar pages land together. A second small model then gives each group a short name.

Letting one big language model do all of it was slower and no better: on a test of 43 tabs it took 18.6 seconds, against 2.5 for this split. Each model does the part it is good at, so Organize is fast enough to press without thinking.

Where else it works

Anything that piles up and never gets sorted: downloads, screenshots, saved links.

What’s next

  • Organize while you browse. New tabs find their group as they open, so the list never piles up.
  • Search every tab by meaning. The same model that groups pages can find “that article about bread hydration” across hundreds of tabs.
  • Prompts that need taste. “By site” and named lists already work. Requests like “work vs fun” are next.
